Does this box have wildcard subdomains?
That is: anything.<the box's hostname> reaches this box, whatever you put in front of the dot.
▾ how do I check?
In a browser on the machine you will actually be using, open anything.<the box's hostname> — literally the word "anything", or any word you like. If it reaches this box, the answer is yes.
Yes — no tunnel needed.
swe-swe serves every pane on that one port, each on its own subdomain. Adds one line to the script:
SWE_PUBLIC_HOSTNAME=the-box.example.com
Nothing to run, nothing to paste, no once-per-boot steps.
No — a tunnel is the only route.
Adds the tunnel setup plus the once-per-boot browser steps below. The tunnel gives you wildcard subdomains of its own hostname, and hands your ports back on those.
needs a tunnel server: docs link, or a how-to panel? undecided
Two limits on the “Yes” answer, stated rather than hidden.
1. Plain web addresses only. For a secure address (the padlock), something in front of this box has to do the encryption and pass the traffic on, keeping the address it was asked for. swe-swe does not do it itself, and a wildcard certificate — one that covers every subdomain at once — is a different and slower thing to obtain.
2. Only on a box with no Docker, so question 1 has to be ticked too. With Docker, the traffic router in front cannot match wildcard subdomains.
