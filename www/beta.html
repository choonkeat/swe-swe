swe-swe serves every pane on that one port, each on its own subdomain. No tunnel, nothing to paste, no steps after boot.

Two limits, stated rather than hidden.

1. Plain web addresses only. For a secure address (the padlock), something in front of this machine has to do the encryption and pass the traffic on, keeping the address it was asked for. swe-swe does not do it itself, and a wildcard certificate — one that covers every subdomain at once — is a different and slower thing to obtain.

2. Only on a machine without Docker, so the second option at the top has to be the one you picked. With Docker, the traffic router in front cannot match wildcard subdomains.

You picked Docker at the top, so this is not available. Pick the option below and use a tunnel, or pick "git and the coding agents" at the top.

A tunnel is then the only route. It gives you wildcard subdomains of its own hostname and hands your ports back on those. It needs a tunnel server to connect to, which is a separate thing to run — see the docs. Setting it up is the three steps below the script.

Run this

Needs swe-swe 2.38.0 or newer.

Then, once per boot, in your browser

About 30 seconds. None of this is a command, so there is nothing to copy.

  1. Open the address your host gives you, and log in with your password.
  2. Gear icon, then Settings. In the "Tunnel secrets" box, paste your tunnel lines. Apply.
  3. The strip at the top of the page shows "connecting", then a link. Open that link. Every pane works there, and it is the same link every boot.
